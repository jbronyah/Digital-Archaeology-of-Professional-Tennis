# Follow-up findings: tactical evolution and archival selection

Windows: 1995–2004 versus 2015–2025 unless otherwise specified. Sources: Jeff Sackmann / Tennis Abstract and MCP contributors (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0).

## Interpretation

- **Net play changed form:** men’s Wimbledon has far fewer serve-and-volley attempts and more non-serve approaches. However, the share of explicitly recorded approaches occurring on shot 4 or later does not clearly increase. The evidence supports a change in entry type more strongly than a claim that players now wait longer to construct an approach.
- **The plus-one story depends on the denominator:** the large increase in overall forehand plus-one share is largely associated with replacing net shots. Restricting to baseline shot types leaves a much smaller, uncertain Wimbledon change.
- **Convergence is dimension-specific:** Wimbledon rally distributions become more similar to Australian Open and Roland Garros in this selected sample; the US Open contrast is inconclusive. Modern same-player comparisons still show shorter Wimbledon service rallies.
- **Selection is substantial:** early charting concentrates on late rounds and a few players. The men’s Wimbledon net-entry changes survive late-round restriction, round balancing, and excluding the five most-charted players. Only one player meets the strict Wimbledon bridge-player criterion, so that check cannot establish a general within-player historical effect.
